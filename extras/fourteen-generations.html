Afterword

Up the river

Joshua Treat kept the armory at Fort Pownall until the fort was pulled down in 1775, so that its guns would not fall to the British. By then he had already done the thing the fort was built to make possible: he had stayed.

His eldest son, Joshua Jr., raised the first log house, the first sawmill, and the first vessel at Marsh Bay, nine miles upriver, in what became Frankfort. His half-brother Robert went further still and set up a shop at the head of the tide, where Bangor now stands. The Treats of Frankfort and Winterport, the lobster canner, the U.S. Treasurer, and a certain judge in New Hampshire all trace back to the point you have been walking.

Fourteen generations after Richard Treat left Pitminster, the family still climbs this hill.

A fan-made family history project. Landscape, buildings and people are stylized; the names, dates and events in the journal are from the published record. The Penobscot Nation lives today at Indian Island, Maine.
